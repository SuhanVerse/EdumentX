\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{\numberline{}Abstract}
Finding a qualified and trustworthy home tutor in the Kathmandu Valley is mostly an informal and unorganized process. Parents and students usually depend on word-of-mouth recommendations, social media groups, or agency bureaus that charge high broker fees. These traditional methods lack credential verification, distance-based search, and structured scheduling. Existing educational platforms in Nepal mostly focus on online video classes or general classifieds, leaving a clear gap for localized home tutor discovery.

EdumentX is a mobile application developed using React Native and Expo SDK 54 to help students and parents find verified home tutors in their nearby areas. The platform provides an interactive map interface using Google Maps with pin clustering, proximity ranking based on the Haversine distance formula, and an AI-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) chatbot that helps users find tutors using natural language questions. To ensure safety and credibility, tutors go through an identity verification process where citizenship documents and profile photos are checked through optical character recognition (OCR) and face validation before approval. The application supports monthly enrollment requests with weekly time-slot scheduling, group tuition batches, in-app direct messaging, ratings and reviews, and a Pro subscription tier through the eSewa digital payment sandbox. The backend is built using Firebase Authentication, Cloud Firestore, and Supabase Edge Functions operating within free tier quotas. The system was tested through 64 automated unit tests, 21 design-token lint tests, and seven Firestore security rule test suites, demonstrating a practical and cost-effective mobile platform for home tutor discovery in Kathmandu Valley.\\

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} \textit{Location-Based Services, Tutor Discovery, React Native, Firebase Firestore, Haversine Distance, Retrieval-Augmented Generation, eSewa Payment.}

\section{Background}
Education is one of the most important foundations for personal growth and social progress. Around the world, families invest heavily in their children's education, recognizing that formal schooling alone may not always meet every student's learning needs. When students struggle with certain subjects or need dedicated preparation for important board examinations, many families turn to private tutoring as a way to fill academic gaps \cite{Bray2007Shadow}. This practice of hiring tutors outside of regular school hours has become widespread globally, as parents seek additional academic support to help their children perform better \cite{Bray2012Shadow}.

Private tutoring, commonly referred to as shadow education, operates as a parallel academic system alongside formal schooling \cite{Kim2019Shadow}. This supplementary education has grown particularly fast in developing countries, where mainstream school resources vary widely and student-to-teacher ratios remain high. In Nepal, supplementary tuition plays a major role in student preparation, particularly for national board assessments such as the Secondary Education Examination (SEE) and Grade 11/12 (+2) examinations under the National Examination Board. Thapa's study on private and public school competition in Nepal highlights how supplementary tutoring has become an established practice, with approximately 68 percent of Grade 10 students receiving private tutoring to reinforce their classroom learning \cite{Thapa2013Private}.

At the same time, Nepal has experienced rapid urbanization over the past decade. According to the National Population and Housing Census 2021, the overall literacy rate reached 76.3 percent among the population aged five years and above, while the urban population share increased substantially to 66.17 percent \cite{CBS2023}. In urban centers like the Kathmandu Valley, dual-income households have become common. Working parents face strict time limitations in supervising their children's daily coursework and actively search for trusted, qualified home tutors who can deliver focused one-on-one instruction directly at their residence.

Despite this growing demand, Nepal's tutoring market remains fragmented, informal, and unorganized. Parents and students typically rely on word-of-mouth recommendations from friends and relatives, local notice boards, or unstructured social media groups to find tutors. These informal methods lack credential verification, distance-based filtering, and structured scheduling workflows. Furthermore, intermediary tuition agencies charge heavy upfront commissions from independent tutors (often 50\% or more of the first month's payment) while providing zero accountability for ongoing session delivery or tutor safety.

With smartphone penetration in Nepal exceeding 70 percent and internet connectivity expanding across urban centers, the digital infrastructure exists to address these problems through a dedicated mobile application. EdumentX is developed to solve these systemic inefficiencies by providing a transparent, location-first home tutor discovery ecosystem for the Kathmandu Valley. The platform enables students and parents to find nearby verified tutors through an interactive map, enroll in structured monthly plans, and receive personalized recommendations through an AI-powered RAG chatbot.

\section{Problem Statement}
The process of finding a home tutor in Nepal involves several practical difficulties for both students and tutors:
\begin{itemize}
\item Lack of Verification and Trust: Parents cannot easily check whether a tutor is qualified, verified, or trustworthy before inviting them into their home. Most families rely on informal contacts, which limits their choices and provides no assurance of teaching quality or safety.
\item No Proximity-Based Search: Home tutoring requires physical travel. Existing classifieds and social media posts list only general area names without actual distance or map location, leading to frequent cancellations when tutors realize the commute is impractical.
\item Unorganized Communication and Scheduling: Inquiries are usually handled through unstructured phone calls or messaging chats, with no organized records of time slots, subject requirements, or request statuses.
\item High Agency Brokerage Fees: Tuition bureaus charge high upfront commissions from independent tutors (such as university students and part-time educators) while offering no persistent platform for reputation building or direct student-tutor relationships.
\end{itemize}

EdumentX addresses these problems by providing an interactive map with clustered tutor pins, an AI-assisted tutor verification system, a natural language RAG chatbot, slot-based weekly scheduling, and transparent multi-axis reviews.

\section{Objectives}
The main objectives of this project are:
\begin{itemize}
\item To develop a location-based mobile application that allows students and parents to discover nearby home tutors in the Kathmandu Valley using interactive map search and a natural language AI chatbot.
\item To implement a secure tutor verification system alongside transparent review mechanisms, helping parents make safe and informed hiring decisions.
\item To provide a complete management system that streamlines monthly enrollments, slot-based scheduling, group tuition batches, and direct in-app communication between tutors and students.
\end{itemize}

\section{Scope}
The scope of the EdumentX project includes:
\begin{itemize}
\item Target Users: Students, parents, independent home tutors, and platform administrators.
\item Geographic Focus: Kathmandu Valley (Kathmandu, Lalitpur, and Bhaktapur), within standard Nepal coordinates ($26.35^\circ\text{N}$ to $30.45^\circ\text{N}$, $80.06^\circ\text{E}$ to $88.20^\circ\text{E}$).
\item Service Type: Physical in-person home tuition matching; online video classes are not included.
\item Academic Levels: School and college students covering Grade 1--10, SEE, +2 Science/Management, and undergraduate levels.
\item Mobile Platform: Android mobile application built using React Native with Expo SDK 54.
\item Discovery Methods: Interactive map search with proximity ranking and conversational AI chatbot.
\item Enrollment Model: Monthly enrollment plans (1, 3, 6, or 12 months) with weekly time-slot selection ($day:slot$).
\item Verification: Blue Tick badge awarded after document verification (citizenship and photo review).
\item Group Learning: Group tuition batches (2 to 6 seats) created by tutors for shared, cost-effective learning.
\item Subscription: Pro Tutor tier with eSewa sandbox payment for increased student capacity and visibility.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
Recent academic literature and commercial systems have explored digital tutoring and local services. This section reviews relevant systems and identifies the specific gaps that EdumentX aims to address.

A 2022 study presented at ASCILITE introduced MENTOR, an intelligent mobile peer tutoring platform designed for higher education institutions \cite{Chung2022MENTOR}. MENTOR matched peer tutors with students based on subject strengths and academic availability. While it demonstrated the value of mobile platforms for tutoring coordination, it did not include any location-based features such as map visualization or proximity ranking. The matching algorithm operated solely on academic criteria without considering the physical commute between participants.

Sharma et al. (2026) described a Local Tutor Finder Application built using Flutter and Firebase \cite{Sharma2026LocalTutor}. The application integrated Google Maps for proximity-based tutor search and calculated straight-line distances using the Haversine formula. Students could filter tutors by subject, rate, and city. However, the system lacked automated document verification and intelligent recommendation features, requiring students to browse listings manually. The authors noted that AI-based recommendations were left as future scope.

Shari et al. (2025) proposed iTeachU, an AI-driven tutor matching platform utilizing geolocation integration within the Melaka region of Malaysia \cite{Shari2025iTeachU}. The platform combined content-based filtering with the K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) algorithm to rank tutors by subject preferences and physical distance. It was built using Flutter for the frontend and Flask for the backend. While effective, the user interface was rigid, relying strictly on fixed dropdown menus without conversational natural language support.

In Nepal, online rental platforms such as KothaBhada, GharBheti, and BasoBaas have shown the effectiveness of digital discovery for real estate. However, these systems are built specifically for rental properties. For home tutoring, users still rely on unvetted Facebook groups, WhatsApp chats, or agency bureaus that offer no schedule management, verified ratings, or map visualization. \subsection{Location-Based Services and Haversine Distance Formula}
Location-Based Services (LBS) deliver context-aware information using a device's real-time geographic position obtained through GPS or network triangulation. In EdumentX, tutor distances are calculated using the Haversine great-circle formula \cite{Sinnott1984Haversine}, which calculates the shortest surface distance over the spherical Earth ($R \approx 6{,}371\text{ km}$):
\begin{equation}
a = \sin^2\!\left(\frac{\Delta\phi}{2}\right) + \cos(\phi_1)\cdot\cos(\phi_2)\cdot\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\Delta\lambda}{2}\right)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
c = 2\cdot\operatorname{atan2}\!\left(\sqrt{a},\,\sqrt{1-a}\right), \qquad d = R \cdot c
\end{equation}
where $\phi_1, \phi_2$ are latitudes in radians, $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$ are longitudes in radians, $\Delta\phi = \phi_2 - \phi_1$, $\Delta\lambda = \lambda_2 - \lambda_1$, and $d$ is the calculated distance in kilometers. For the 1 to 20 km distances typical in the Kathmandu Valley, this formula provides accurate proximity calculation.

\subsection{Geofencing and Radius-Based Filtering}
Before executing the trigonometric Haversine formula, EdumentX applies a rectangular bounding box pre-filter to narrow down candidate tutors and optimize mobile device performance \cite{Shevchenko2023Geofencing}. For a given search radius $r$ and student position $(\phi_s, \lambda_s)$:
\begin{equation}
\Delta\phi_{\max} = \frac{r}{R}\cdot\frac{180}{\pi}, \qquad \Delta\lambda_{\max} = \frac{r}{R\cdot\cos(\phi_s)}\cdot\frac{180}{\pi}
\end{equation}
Only tutors whose coordinates fall inside this bounding box proceed to the exact Haversine distance check, keeping query execution fast.

\subsection{Nearest-Neighbor Ranking by Geographic Distance}
The tutor recommendation problem is treated as a one-dimensional nearest-neighbor search over geographic distance, following the classical K-nearest neighbors formulation of Cover and Hart \cite{Cover1967KNN}. Candidate tutors inside the geofence bounding box (Section 2.2.4) are ranked by their exact Haversine distance from the student's location, in ascending order, so the closest tutors appear first:
\begin{equation}
R = \operatorname{sort}_{d}\left(\{t_1, t_2, \dots, t_k\}\right), \quad d(t_i) = d_{\text{haversine}}(s, t_i)
\end{equation}
On top of this ordering, the platform applies a stable tier promotion: Pro-subscribed tutors are moved to the top of the list while preserving the original distance order within each tier. This keeps the ranking transparent and explainable — a student always sees why a tutor appears where they do — while still giving subscribers the visibility they pay for.

\subsection{Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and Semantic Search}
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is an architecture that combines information retrieval with large language model generation to produce responses grounded in private, real-time data rather than static model weights \cite{Lewis2021RAG}. In EdumentX, RAG parses student natural language queries into structured parameters (subject, grade, budget, locality), fetches matching active tutor profiles from Firestore, and injects them as factual context into the Groq LLM prompt. This approach prevents model hallucinations and ensures recommendations reflect live platform listings \cite{Swacha2025RAG}.

\subsection{Mobile Application Framework (React Native and Expo)}
React Native enables cross-platform mobile development from a unified TypeScript codebase. Expo SDK 54 provides a managed native toolchain with pre-configured modules for device location (\texttt{expo-location}), native camera and image pickers (\texttt{expo-image-picker}), file system storage, and native Google Maps rendering (\texttt{expo-maps}).

\subsection{Multi-Axis Rating Aggregation and Anti-Tampering}
To prevent rating bias and unilateral manipulation, EdumentX breaks tutor ratings into five qualitative dimensions: Communication, Subject Knowledge, Punctuality, Clarity, and Engagement. When an enrolled student submits a review, a Firestore transaction recalculates running averages atomically:
\begin{equation}
R_{\text{new}} = \frac{R_{\text{old}} \cdot N_{\text{old}} + S_{\text{new}}}{N_{\text{old}} + 1}
\end{equation}

\chapter{Methodology}

This chapter details the problem-solving methodology implemented in EdumentX, covering the system development framework, technical architecture, and module-level implementation across the application lifecycle.

\section{System Architecture and Implementation Framework}

\subsection{Mobile Client Architecture}
The mobile application is developed in React Native (v0.81.5) using Expo SDK 54:
\begin{itemize}
\item Design System: NativeWind 4.2 provides universal styling through Tailwind CSS classes mapped to native primitives without runtime styling overhead. Custom ESLint rules enforce design token compliance across all components.
\item File-Based Routing: Expo Router v6 manages flat stack navigation under \texttt{src/app/}, featuring layout guards in \texttt{\_layout.tsx} that protect private routes based on authentication and verification state.
\item Fluid Animations: React Native Reanimated and \texttt{expo-haptics} deliver 100ms spring animations for bottom sheets, modal drawers, and interactive buttons.
\item Client-Side State: Zustand stores manage session state, user roles, and recent conversation history.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Backend Services and Serverless Compute}
The backend architecture is structured around modular cloud services:
\begin{itemize}
\item Firebase Core: Manages OAuth2 tokens and Cloud Firestore document persistence with rule-level access control.
\item Supabase Edge Functions: Deno-based serverless endpoints executing AI RAG inference (\texttt{/chat}), eSewa HMAC-SHA256 order signing (\texttt{/create-esewa-order}), and OCR verification (\texttt{/verify-identity}).
\item Supabase Storage: Object storage partitioned into \texttt{public-avatars} (5 MB max) and \texttt{private-verification-docs} (25 MB max).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{AI Tutor Verification Module}
To ensure platform safety, tutors must verify their identity before their profiles become public. The tutor uploads photos of their citizenship document and a self-portrait to Supabase Storage. A Supabase Edge Function then uses AI (Groq Vision OCR) to extract the holder's name from the document and compare it against the applicant's profile name, while the portrait is checked for a real human face. Submissions with a high confidence score are automatically approved, while lower scores are flagged for manual review by an administrator.

\subsection{Tutor Discovery and Map Module}
The application provides an interactive map for students to discover nearby tutors. Using the \texttt{expo-location} library, the app fetches the user's current coordinates and displays tutors as custom pins on the map. The Haversine formula is used to calculate the exact distance between the student and the tutor. To ensure smooth performance, the map clusters nearby pins together when zoomed out.

The tutor can then review the request and choose to accept, decline, or reschedule it. Once accepted, the student is added to the tutor's active roster, and the tutor gains secure access to the student's exact location for home tuition. \subsection{Group Batches Management Module}
Tutors have the option to group multiple students into a single batch, ranging from 2 to 6 students. This feature allows tutors to offer shared classes at a reduced monthly rate. Tutors can invite their actively enrolled students into these batches. A visual capacity ring in the UI dynamically shows the remaining available seats in each batch.

\subsection{In-App Direct Messaging Module}
A built-in chat system allows seamless communication between students and tutors without requiring third-party messaging apps. The module generates a unique, deterministic conversation ID for each user pair to maintain privacy. The chat interface supports real-time text messaging, unread message badges, and read receipts to track message status.

\subsection{Pro Tier and eSewa Payment Module}
Tutors can upgrade to a Pro subscription to increase their maximum student capacity. The application integrates with eSewa, Nepal's leading digital wallet, to process these payments. When a plan is selected, the app generates a pending transaction and redirects the user to the eSewa portal. Upon returning to the app, a backend Edge Function verifies the payment status directly with the eSewa server.

\subsection{Ratings and Reviews Module}
To build a reliable community, students can review their tutors after completing their classes. The rating system covers five specific categories: Communication, Subject Knowledge, Punctuality, Clarity, and Engagement. The platform restricts review access only to officially enrolled students, preventing spam and fake ratings. The average rating is automatically recalculated using an atomic database transaction.

\subsection{AI Chatbot Module}
The application includes a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) chatbot to help students find tutors using natural language. A student can type a prompt like "Find a science tutor in Baneshwor." The AI extracts the intent, searches the Firestore database for matching profiles, and returns a conversational response with direct, clickable links to the recommended tutors.

\subsection{Admin Dashboard Module}
A dedicated administrative portal provides an overview of key platform metrics, such as total registered users and active tutors. The primary function of the admin dashboard is managing the tutor verification queue. Administrators can manually inspect flagged citizenship documents and photos, and officially approve or reject tutor applications.

\item \textbf{Privacy}: Exact location data and home addresses of students must remain hidden until an enrollment request is explicitly accepted by the tutor.
\item \textbf{Reliability}: The mobile application must handle sudden network drops gracefully and display appropriate error messages without crashing.
\item \textbf{Usability}: The user interface should be clean, intuitive, and navigable with minimal effort for users of all ages.
\end{itemize}

The architecture is divided into three primary layers:
\begin{enumerate}

\section{AI RAG Chatbot Workflow}
To provide an intuitive search experience, EdumentX implements a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline. Figure~\ref{fig:ragworkflow} illustrates how the system processes natural language queries to deliver accurate, context-aware tutor recommendations.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\safeincludegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{RAG.png}
\caption{RAG-Based AI Chatbot Architecture}
\label{fig:ragworkflow}
\end{figure}

The chatbot processes queries through the following pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Query Input:} The student submits a natural language question (e.g., "Need a physics tutor in Baneshwor") alongside their recent chat history for context.
\item \textbf{Parameter Extraction:} A specialized Groq LLM prompt extracts key search parameters such as subject, grade level, maximum budget, and preferred location.
\item \textbf{Database Retrieval:} The extracted parameters are used to query Cloud Firestore and the pgvector database to fetch highly relevant candidate tutor profiles.
\item \textbf{Prompt Construction:} The retrieved tutor details are injected into a final system prompt, grounding the AI's knowledge in real, current database records.
\item \textbf{Response Generation:} The model synthesizes a conversational, personalized reply recommending the best-matching tutors based on the student's constraints.
\item \textbf{Card Rendering:} The mobile application parses the response and renders interactive UI cards, allowing the student to click directly through to a tutor's full profile.
\end{enumerate}

\chapter{Conclusions}

This project set out to solve a problem we had all experienced in our own neighbourhoods: finding a dependable home tutor in the Kathmandu Valley still depends on word-of-mouth, pamphlets, and unverified social media posts. Over the minor project we built EdumentX, an
Android application that replaces that guesswork with a searchable, verified marketplace
connecting students directly with tutors. All of the objectives set out in our proposal were
met, and some grew beyond what we first planned after the mid-term review.

Our circumstances shaped the technical decisions as much as our preferences did. With no institutional funding or international payment method, we had to build on free tiers, and that constraint became one of the most useful lessons of the project. Firebase Authentication and Cloud Firestore handle identity and the primary database; their real-time listeners drive the live tutor dashboards and enrollment updates without any custom server. Object storage, serverless business logic and vector search for the chatbot run on Supabase. The platform costs nothing per month to operate at demo scale, and holding that line forced us to understand each service instead of reaching for paid shortcuts.

The features themselves came together into a coherent product. Students find tutors on an interactive Google Maps view where pins are clustered for performance and ranked by
real Haversine distance from their location. The retrieval-augmented chatbot answers plain
English questions from live database records rather than invented suggestions. Enrollment
requests pass through an atomic transaction that respects capacity, group batches let
one tutor teach many students together, and messaging keeps both sides coordinated
inside the app. The eSewa integration, completed in the sandbox, showed that a local digital
wallet can drive a subscription end to end.

The part we are most satisfied with is tutor verification. A vision model reads the uploaded citizenship certificate and compares the holder's name with the profile, while the photograph is checked for a real human face. Clear passes are approved automatically; anything doubtful goes to the admin queue with an AI pre-screen verdict attached. Building this taught us how fragile AI features are in practice: a genuine certificate with one misspelled letter fell below our match threshold, and the vision model leaked its internal reasoning into outputs until we constrained it at the API level. Finally, we verified our own work seriously: sixty-four unit tests cover the scheduling, distance and capacity logic, twenty-one lint tests keep the design tokens consistent, and seven Firestore rule suites confirm that location privacy and chat access behave as specified. Together with manual testing on physical devices, this gives us confidence that EdumentX is a working platform, not merely a demonstration.

\chapter{Limitations and Future Enhancement}

\section{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item iOS Platform Verification: The native iOS project has been generated from the shared codebase and compiles, but systematic testing on physical iPhone devices has not been carried out; all functional validation was performed on Android. Map rendering on iOS would also fall back to Apple Maps, as Google Maps credentials are currently provisioned for Android only.
\item eSewa Sandbox: The payment integration runs in the eSewa test environment (\texttt{EPAYTEST}). Production deployment requires a registered business merchant account.
\item Service Coverage: Tutor discovery content is concentrated in the Kathmandu Valley, where seed data exists, and geocoding relies on the keyless Nominatim service, which enforces a strict fair-use rate limit of roughly one request per second.
\item In-Person Focus: The platform is designed specifically for physical home tutoring; online video classes are not included.
\item Push Notification Delivery: Background message notifications are wired through a Cloud Function trigger and Expo push tokens; delivery behaviour still varies across device vendors because of aggressive battery optimization on some Android phones.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future Enhancement}
\begin{itemize}
\item iOS Release: Complete on-device quality assurance of the existing iOS build, provision Google Maps for iOS, and distribute the app to testers through TestFlight.
\item Production Payments: Integrate live eSewa and Khalti merchant payment gateways with automated escrow payout.
\item Online Tutoring Mode: Extend session delivery beyond physical home visits by adding integrated video calls and digital learning materials.
\item Nepali Language Support: Add Nepali language translation for the user interface and AI chatbot replies.
\item Tutor Analytics: Provide tutors with dashboard charts showing profile views, search appearances, and subject demand trends in their area.
\end{itemize}
